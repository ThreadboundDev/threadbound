# Lakeside junction concepts

These are proposals for the section between the cave approach, lower route and the already modeled tower. The user permits modest local shape changes. These images do not replace collision, game scenes or approved assets.

## A — Netmaker's Landing

One small cottage on the central rock terrace, with a simple uncovered dock on the upside-down-U footprint. This keeps the most open space and has the smallest new building set.

## B — Two-House Fishing Court

A low net-repair workshop and a modest fisher's house at staggered elevations, with an open working terrace between them. The inverted U remains a simple dock. This is the recommended starting direction: enough buildings to read as a small village without another large landmark competing with the tower.

## C — Covered Boat Slip

The inverted U becomes an open-sided roofed boat shelter. The central mass is a workshop partly embedded in the shoreline cliff, with more unroofed working ground above. This puts more architectural emphasis on the lower dock.

## Shared direction

- Medieval Japanese fishing village: slate-blue tile roofs, walnut framing, warm plaster, stone footings and sparse fishing-work details.
- Cave stays left; the central raised terrace, lower passage and existing right-hand tower remain spatial anchors.
- Only the cropped local edge of the tower belongs in this map section; the entire tower should not be shrunk to fit.
- The still lake is environmental context. No exact game water elevation is established by the screenshot.
- Refine platform clearances against the actual map after choosing a composition.
- Functions such as workshop/house/boat shelter are proposed visual roles, not new canon or gameplay systems.

The built-in ImageGen prompts are in generation-prompts.md. References are preserved under references/.

## Render review

All three concepts retain the cave/central terrace/dock/tower relationship, with different amounts of occupation. B provides the clearest sequence from cave to low workshop, raised house and tower. C gives the dock the strongest separate identity. Its generated lower boardwalk bridges a gap present in the blockout; keep or restore that gap deliberately during any later layout pass. These are composition studies rather than collision templates.
