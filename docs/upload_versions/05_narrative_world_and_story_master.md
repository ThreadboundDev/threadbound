## Guardrails

- Eryndor is a living world-consciousness and the primary narrator.
- Thought is the First Weaver, a self-aware fragment of Eryndor who entered creation willingly.
- The primordial Threads were originally unified within Eryndor and were never meant to be possessed.
- The King, Monk, and Scholar discovered and took the Threads, becoming the Monarch, Hermit, and Sage.
- The Reclaimers are native beings uplifted by Thought to protect the Threads.
- The Weaver was created by the Three to stabilize the wound caused by the separation of the Threads.
- The Weaver is not the creator of the world and did not shatter the Loom.
- The Threadborne emerged from the wound created by the separation of the Threads and represents possibility.
- The Follower is Thought, but this is secret-route knowledge and should not be revealed through normal gameplay.

When uncertain, preserve existing canon rather than introducing new cosmological entities, primordial forces, creator figures, or retcons.

# Cosmology & Origins – Threadbound

## Eryndor

Eryndor is not merely a world.

She is a living consciousness.

Ancient and patient, Eryndor serves as:

- the canvas
- the witness
- the memory of the world

Eryndor is not omnipotent.

Though aware, she does not dictate reality.

The world was created with free will woven into it, and Eryndor cannot simply override choice without violating the very nature of creation.

This limitation becomes critical after the Threads are separated.

---

## Thought — The First Weaver

Before the Weaver of the current age, there was Thought.

Thought is not separate from Eryndor.

He is:

- a fragment of Eryndor
- the first self-aware consciousness
- the first Weaver

Thought emerged from Eryndor as awareness and longing.

He did not create from domination or command.

He created because he did not wish to be alone.

Together:

- Eryndor
- Thought
- and the primordial Threads

wove the world.

Thought is cosmically young.

Though he experiences immense spans of time, he matures differently than mortal beings.

Thousands of years may pass while he remains comparatively youthful in wisdom.

His defining trait is not perfection—

but relational love and immaturity.

He believes freedom is inherently good.

He cannot imagine theft or domination.

This belief shapes the tragedy that follows.

---

## The Primordial Threads

The Threads are not sentient beings.

They are primordial forces.

They represent foundational principles woven through reality:

- Power
- Balance
- Essence

Originally:

- the Threads were united
- diffused through Eryndor
- and inseparable from creation

They were never intended to be possessed.

Nor were they meant to be separated from:

- one another
- Eryndor
- or the harmony of the world.

---

## Original Harmony

At the beginning:

Eryndor + Thought + Threads

formed living harmony.

Not perfection.

Not utopia.

Harmony.

Choice existed.

Imperfection existed.

But the world remained whole.

---

## Thought Enters Creation

Having woven the world, Thought desires more than observation.

He wishes:

- to witness
- to participate
- to belong

He voluntarily enters creation.

Not to study it as an experiment—

but to live within it.

He relinquishes stewardship of the Threads.

This is not abandonment.

It is trust.

He assumes no one would seek or seize such power.

The idea of theft is foreign to him.

This choice reflects both his love and his youth.

---

## The Discovery of the Threads

Ages pass.

Civilizations grow.

The peoples of Eryndor study the woven nature of reality.

Magic flows through the world without depletion.

Gradually, scholars, mystics, and rulers conclude:

there must be a primordial source.

Three individuals ultimately uncover this truth:

- a great ruler and protector
- a contemplative spiritual figure
- a seeker of knowledge

They later become:

- Monarch
- Hermit
- Sage

Their discovery is not born from evil.

They seek understanding and stewardship.

But the Threads are taken and separated.

This wounds creation itself.

---

## The Wound

The separation of the Threads causes a cosmological injury.

Not immediate destruction—

but fracture.

The Threads become concentrated into mortal vessels.

And because primordial forces are incomplete when isolated:

their bearers begin to change.

The corruption is not malevolent possession.

It is the result of finite beings holding infinite principles.

---

## The Unbound Thread

The wound calls forth response.

Not prophecy.

Not destiny.

But instinctive correction.

Whether consciously or unconsciously:

- Eryndor
- Thought
- and the Threads

act together.

One loose thread emerges.

Untuned.

Unclaimed.

Unbound.

This becomes:

The Threadborne.

The first being untouched by divided power.

Not a chosen savior—

but possibility itself.

---

# Narrative Voice & Lore Delivery – Threadbound

## Eryndor as Narrator

Eryndor serves as the primary narrative voice.

She is:

- memory
- witness
- world-consciousness

Her voice may appear:

- at save points
- meditation sites
- key locations
- moments of reflection

Delivery should feel:

- quiet
- reflective
- intimate
- poetic

Not instructional.

Not verbose.

The world speaks.

---

## Meditation / Reflection Sites

Inspired by reflective spaces.

Potential names:

- Memory Knots
- Woven Shrines
- Meditation Looms
- Resting Threads

At these spaces:

- camera may zoom
- audio quiets
- Eryndor speaks

Lore delivery should occur sparingly.

The goal is:

reflection rather than exposition dumping.

---

## Division of Narrative Roles

### Eryndor

Speaks of:

- memory
- place
- history
- emotional truth

She contextualizes the world.

---

### The Follower / Thought

Speaks of:

- identity
- philosophy
- freedom
- becoming

He gives:

- cryptic guidance
- questions
- personal reflection

He should never become an exposition machine.

The player should feel:

"He knows something I do not."

---

## Hidden Truth

The player may know:

Thought existed.

The player may not know:

Thought is the Follower.

This truth remains hidden until secret conditions are met.

The Follower is not merely mysterious.

He is:

The First Weaver.

# Cosmology Timeline – Threadbound

## 1. Eryndor Exists

Eryndor is the living world-consciousness.

She is ancient, aware, and alive.

She is not omnipotent.

She does not impose control over creation.

---

## 2. Thought Emerges

From Eryndor, Thought becomes self-aware.

Thought is:

- a fragment of Eryndor
- the first awareness
- the First Weaver

He is young on a cosmic scale.

He desires companionship.

---

## 3. The World Is Woven

Thought, Eryndor, and the primordial Threads weave the world together.

The Threads are:

- Power
- Balance
- Essence

They are not sentient.

They are forces.

They are meant to remain united and diffused through Eryndor.

---

## 4. Thought Enters Creation

Thought does not want to merely observe the world.

He wants to be part of it.

He enters creation willingly.

He leaves the Threads unattended, not through malice, but through innocence and trust.

He cannot yet imagine that anyone would seek to possess them.

---

## 5. The Reclaimers Are Uplifted

As Thought's final great work, he uplifts native creatures of Eryndor into sentience.

These become the Reclaimers.

They are given society, memory, and sacred purpose.

Their duty:

> Protect the Threads so that no one meddles with choice.

---

## 6. Civilizations Study Magic

Over long ages, the peoples of Eryndor study the world's magic.

They discover that magic flows through reality without depletion.

They conclude that there must be a primordial source.

---

## 7. The Three Discover the Threads

Three mortals discover the Threads:

- a ruler/protector
- a contemplative mediator
- a scholar/seeker

They do not begin as villains.

They believe they can understand or steward the source.

---

## 8. The Threads Are Taken

The Three take the Threads.

They become immortal.

The Threads are separated from:

- each other
- Eryndor
- their natural harmony

This causes a wound in creation.

---

## 9. The Reclaimers Fail

The Reclaimers fail to prevent the separation.

This failure becomes their defining trauma.

Their original purpose hardens into obsession.

---

## 10. The Three Create the Weaver

Before they are fully consumed by the Threads, the Three realize they cannot trust themselves.

Together, in a moment of clarity, they create the Weaver.

The Weaver is meant to be a neutral steward.

It exists to govern what the Three can no longer govern.

---

## 11. The Thread Masters Become Consumed

Over centuries or millennia, the Three become more tightly fused with their Threads.

They do not simply hold power.

They become manifestations of isolated principles.

- Monarch becomes Power without restraint
- Hermit becomes Balance without action
- Sage becomes Essence without grounding

They can technically relinquish the Threads, but doing so would mean mortality, loss, withdrawal, and likely death.

They are addicted in a mythic sense.

---

## 12. The Weaver Turns Toward Control

The Weaver tries to stabilize the world.

Over time, it concludes that choice is the source of catastrophe.

It begins to manage freedom.

Then restrict it.

Then control it.

---

## 13. Eryndor Responds

Eryndor feels the wound, but cannot simply override free will.

The Threads strain toward reunion.

Thought recognizes the failure of freedom without guidance.

Together, instinctively or mysteriously, they produce one loose Thread.

---

## 14. The Threadborne Awakens

The Threadborne is:

- untuned
- unclaimed
- untainted
- unbound

They are not a chosen hero.

They are possibility.

They are the first being capable of answering the wound without inheriting a fixed answer.

---

## 15. The Game Begins

The Threadborne awakens into a fractured world.

The central question is no longer:

> Can the world be saved?

It is:

> What should freedom become?

\# Symbolism \& Motifs – Threadbound



Threadbound communicates its themes through:



\- environment

\- mechanics

\- visual identity

\- character interaction



It does not explain meaning directly.



It allows the player to \*\*interpret it through experience\*\*.



\---



\## 🧵 Core Thematic Focus



At its core, Threadbound explores:



\- choice vs predestination

\- identity through action

\- control vs freedom

\- meaning in a fractured world



Every system reinforces these ideas.



\---



\## 🎭 Primary Symbolism



\### 🧶 The Tapestry



\*\*Represents:\*\* Predestination, order, divine structure



\- A world where every thread has a place

\- A system where outcomes are decided before they occur



\---



\### 🪡 The Loom (and its Shattering)



\*\*Represents:\*\* Control, authorship, imposed meaning



\- The act of weaving = dictating reality

\- The breaking of the Loom = rejection of control



\---



\### 🔴 Absorption



\*\*Represents:\*\* Power, domination, transformation



\- Taking threads into oneself

\- Increasing saturation, intensity, and visual complexity



\---



\### ⚪ Sparing



\*\*Represents:\*\* Mercy, restraint, release



\- Refusing to take power

\- Gradual desaturation toward white

\- Simplicity and quiet presence



\---



\### 🐛 The Reclaimers



\*\*Represents:\*\* Restoration, instinct, blind correction



\- Reassembling what was broken

\- Acting without questioning purpose



They may be:



\- preservers of order

\- or enforcers of something outdated



\---



\### 👁️ The Mysterious Follower (Thought)



\*\*Represents:\*\* Conscience, temptation, observation



\- Questions the player’s actions

\- Reflects internal conflict

\- Exists between guidance and manipulation



\---



\### 🧵 The Threadborne



\*\*Represents:\*\* Possibility, agency, undefined identity



\- Not assigned purpose

\- Not bound to a single thread

\- Defined only through action



\---



### Thought

Represents:

* curiosity
* creation
* companionship
* freedom

Thought is the world's first question made manifest.

He creates not from authority, but from longing.

His tragedy is that he trusted freedom without fully understanding the consequences of power.

---

### The Weaver

Represents:

* stewardship
* protection
* control
* unintended consequence

The Weaver begins as an act of wisdom.

Created to stabilize a wounded world, it gradually concludes that freedom itself is dangerous.

Its tragedy is not malice.

It is protection without trust.



\---



\## 🔁 Systemic Symbolism (Gameplay Integration)



Threadbound’s mechanics reinforce its themes:



\---



\### 🎮 Equipment \& Weaving



\- Swapping abilities in real time represents \*\*active decision-making\*\*

\- No locked builds reinforces \*\*fluid identity\*\*



\---



\### 🎨 Color System



\- Saturation = power and influence

\- Desaturation = restraint and release

\- Color blending = layered identity

\- Black/White extremes = ideological endpoints



\---



\### ⚔️ Combat Flow



\- Continuous motion = freedom

\- Adaptation = agency

\- Expression = identity



\---



\## 🌍 Environmental Motifs



Recurring visual elements reinforce meaning:



\- \*\*Frayed cloth\*\* → instability, decay of structure

\- \*\*Glowing threads\*\* → lingering influence of the Tapestry

\- \*\*Stained-glass imagery\*\* → memory, interpretation of truth

\- \*\*Flowing silk\*\* → motion, continuity, balance

\- \*\*Shifting geometry\*\* → uncertainty, perception, illusion



\---



\## 🎭 Narrative Delivery Style



Threadbound avoids direct exposition.



Instead:



\- environments tell stories

\- characters speak in implication

\- mechanics reinforce meaning



The player is never told what to believe.



They are shown the consequences of their actions.



\---



\## 🔥 Core Motif



> You are not following a pattern.



You are creating one.



\---



\## 🧠 Summary



Threadbound’s symbolism is not separate from its systems.



It is embedded within them.



Every action the player takes contributes to:



\- meaning

\- identity

\- and interpretation



\---



> The world does not tell you what it means.



It shows you what your choices create.

\# Story Outline



Threadbound tells a story of identity, control, and consequence through player choice, environmental storytelling, and evolving character relationships.



The narrative is structured to support nonlinear exploration while maintaining a clear emotional and thematic arc.



\---



# Story Outline

## Act I — Awakening

### Opening Cinematic

The player is introduced to Eryndor through narration from the world itself.

Eryndor recounts her earliest memories:

* becoming aware
* the emergence of Thought
* the weaving of creation
* Thought entering the world
* the discovery of the Threads by a King, a Monk, and a Scholar
* the creation of the Weaver
* the continued wound caused by the separation of the Threads

The cinematic ends in the present.

The Weaver gathers loose threads in the Grand Loom.

One thread escapes.

The Loom Blade cuts it free.

As Eryndor wonders what a loose thread could become, the thread falls into the world and forms the Threadborne.

Gameplay begins.

### Awakening

The Threadborne awakens within the Chamber of the First Weave.

They possess no defined identity.

No allegiance.

No predetermined purpose.

They are possibility.



\## 🌍 Act II — The Fractured World



\### 4. Exploration (Nonlinear)



The world opens into three primary regions:



\- \*\*Monarch’s Domain (Red)\*\* — collapsing power structures

\- \*\*Hermit’s Waters (Blue)\*\* — isolated, reflective spaces

\- \*\*Sage’s Libraries (Yellow)\*\* — shifting, uncertain reality



These can be approached in any order.



\---



\### 5. The Mysterious Follower



Throughout exploration, the \*\*Mysterious Follower\*\* appears intermittently.



\- Observes the player’s choices

\- Questions motivations

\- Offers unclear guidance



Their tone evolves based on:



\- absorbed vs spared Thread Masters

\- player progression

\- emerging identity



\---



\### 6. Thread Master Encounters



Each region culminates in a confrontation with a Thread Master.



After each battle, the player must choose:



\#### Spare

\- The Master survives

\- The world reflects hope, continuity, or change

\- Player gains \*\*passive/world-based effects\*\*

\- Visual identity desaturates



\#### Absorb

\- The Master’s thread is taken

\- Player unlocks \*\*equipment (Gloves / Boots / Chest-Head)\*\*

\- The world becomes more hostile or unstable

\- Visual identity intensifies



> These choices are permanent and cumulative.



\---



\## ⚖️ Act III — Convergence



\### 7. World Reaction



As choices accumulate:



\- NPCs react differently

\- Environments subtly shift

\- Traversal paths evolve in tone and expression



The player begins to see the consequences of:



\- taking power

\- refusing power

\- or mixing both



The Follower becomes more direct… and more personal.



\---



\### 8. Optional — The Reclaimers



A hidden path opens under specific conditions.



The \*\*Reclaimers’ Area\*\* represents:



\- rejection of both control and inheritance

\- a space where threads are stripped away



Mechanics may include:



\- temporary loss of abilities

\- high-risk traversal/combat challenges

\- unique rewards tied to extreme endings



\---



\## 🕳️ Act IV — The Loom



\### 9. Return to the Grand Loom



All paths lead back to the shattered Loom.



The world feels:



\- unstable

\- incomplete

\- waiting



The Threadborne arrives not as a hero—



but as something \*\*undefined\*\*.



\---



\### 10. Final Confrontation — The Weaver



The Weaver awaits.



They do not greet the player with hostility, but with recognition.



The fight reflects everything the player has become:



\- Absorb-heavy → raw power vs instability

\- Spare-heavy → precision vs restraint

\- Mixed → adaptive, balanced encounter



RTA-style moments highlight:



\- mastery of systems

\- identity through action

\- final assertion of control (or lack of it)



\---



\## 🎬 Act V — Resolution



\### 11. Endings



Multiple endings (8+) based on:



\- starting affinity

\- absorb vs spare decisions

\- Reclaimer interaction



\### Core Outcome Types



\- \*\*Pure Paths\*\* (single-color dominance)

\- \*\*Hybrid Paths\*\* (mixed identity)

\- \*\*White Path\*\* (spare all — transcendence)

\- \*\*Black Path\*\* (absorb all — collapse/overload)

\- \*\*Reclaimer Endings\*\* (reject system entirely)



\---



\## 🎭 Tone \& Delivery



The story is conveyed through:



\- environment and visual shifts

\- character encounters (especially the Follower)

\- player transformation

\- minimal direct exposition



Dialogue is:



\- sparse

\- intentional

\- interpretive



\---



\## 🔥 Core Narrative Arc



The story does not ask:



> “Can you save the world?”



It asks:



> “What does the world become when you are free to shape it?”



\---



\## 🧵 Final Note



Threadbound is not about a fixed narrative.



It is about:



\- expression

\- consequence

\- and identity



The player does not uncover the story.



They \*\*create it\*\*.

# Opening Cinematic Narration (Revised)

There was a time when the world was... simpler.

At least, I think it was.

Life was young then.

Small things wandered the forests and fields. They ate, slept, fought, and fled. Seasons came and went. Rivers carved their paths. The stars turned overhead.

And I watched.

Though, I did not know that was what I was doing.

Watching.

I did not know many things then.

I did not know what I was.

Or why I was.

I only remember a feeling.

A question.

One that seemed to linger longer each time it appeared.

Can a world wonder?

Can I?

I don't know how long I carried that question.

Long enough, perhaps.

Because eventually, the wondering became something more.

A thought.

No... Thought.

He was a part of me, I think.

Or perhaps I was a part of him.

It's difficult to say where one thing ends and another begins.

Especially after so much time.

What I do remember is that he looked upon the world and saw possibility everywhere.

Where I saw rivers, he saw journeys.

Where I saw mountains, he saw horizons.

Where I saw life, he saw stories.

And he wanted more of them.

So together, we wove.

Not because anything was missing.

Not at first.

But because creation is a strange thing.

Once it begins, it seems to want to continue.

The world grew.

Life grew with it.

And for a while, that was enough.

But Thought was curious.

Perhaps too curious.

Then again...

most wonderful things begin that way.

He wished to know the world he had helped shape.

Not from above.

But from within.

So he went to walk among it.

And the ages passed.

More lives.

More stories.

More questions.

Always questions.

Eventually, others began asking them too.

A King.

A Monk.

A Scholar.

They searched for the source of the world around them.

For the place where its wonders began.

And after much searching...

they found it.

I remember being afraid.

Though I did not understand why.

Not until later.

The keepers failed.

The Threads were taken.

And something changed.

Not all at once.

More like a crack spreading through stone.

Slow.

Quiet.

Easy to miss.

Until suddenly it wasn't.

What had once moved together began to pull apart.

The world endured.

It always does.

But the wound remained.

The King.

The Monk.

The Scholar.

They saw what the Threads were making of them.

And fearing what they might become, they sought another answer.

A keeper.

One who could mend what was fraying.

One who could hold together what others could not.

The Weaver.

For a time, that answer seemed enough.

But time changes everything.

Even good intentions.

Especially good intentions.

And now...

the Weaver still labors.

Still gathers.

Still binds.

Still tries to hold the world together.

Yet somewhere amid the countless threads...

one slipped free.

A small thing, perhaps.

Easy to overlook.

But then...

once, a question became Thought.

I wonder what a loose thread could become.

# Five Answers to Freedom – Threadbound

Threadbound's major forces each answer the same question:

> What should be done with freedom?

None of them are entirely wrong.

None are complete.

Their conflict forms the philosophical foundation of the game.

---

## Eryndor — Life Should Be Allowed to Grow

Eryndor is the living world-consciousness.

She nurtures rather than controls.

She feels the wound caused by the separation of the Threads, but she does not erase choice.

She is not ruler or judge.

She is the world that suffers and remembers.

---

## Thought — Freedom Is Worth the Risk

Thought is the First Weaver.

He created because he did not want to be alone.

He entered creation because he wanted to belong.

In his youth, he believed freedom alone was enough.

After ages of witnessing suffering, he matures.

He learns that freedom needs wisdom.

But he still refuses to rule.

His method is guidance, not control.

---

## The Thread Masters — One Principle Can Solve Everything

The Thread Masters each began with a virtue.

The Threads made those virtues absolute.

### Monarch

Power can protect.

But isolated Power becomes domination.

### Hermit

Balance can preserve harmony.

But isolated Balance becomes paralysis.

### Sage

Essence can reveal truth.

But isolated Essence becomes endless searching.

The Thread Masters are not evil.

They are incomplete answers made immortal.

---

## The Weaver — Freedom Must Be Managed

The Weaver was created to stabilize the damage caused by the separated Threads.

It begins as a solution.

Over time, it concludes that unrestricted choice causes catastrophe.

Its answer is control.

The Weaver is tragic because it is not malicious.

It is trying to save the world by limiting the thing that makes the world alive.

---

## The Reclaimers — Freedom Must Be Protected From Itself

The Reclaimers were uplifted by Thought to protect the Threads and preserve choice.

After failing to stop the Three, they become polarized.

They no longer trust people near primordial power.

Their answer becomes reclamation.

They seek to return the Threads to where they belong, no matter the cost.

---

## The Threadborne — Possibility

The Threadborne is the only major force without a fixed answer.

They are:

- unbound
- untuned
- undefined

They are not Power, Balance, Essence, Control, or Correction.

They are possibility.

The player does not discover the correct answer.

They create one through action.

---

## Core Theme

Threadbound is not asking:

> Which philosophy is correct?

It is asking:

> What do your choices make true?

---

# Endings Revision Notes (Post-Cosmology Update)

## Ending Philosophy

Threadbound's endings are not intended to represent good versus evil.

Instead, they represent commitment to a philosophy and the consequences that follow.

The player is consistently asked the same question throughout the game:

> What should be done with power once it is obtained?

The ending reached is determined not only by the player's choices, but by the consistency of those choices.

\---

# Category I - Inconsistent Endings

## White Path Failure

Requirements:

* Spare all Thread Masters
* Absorb the Reclaimer

Outcome:
The player spent the game releasing power only to claim it at the final moment.
The Reclaimers judge the Threadborne inconsistent.
The journey ends.

## Black Path Failure

Requirements:

* Absorb all Thread Masters
* Spare the Reclaimer

Outcome:
The player spent the game accumulating power only to reject it at the final moment.
The Reclaimers judge the Threadborne inconsistent.
The journey ends.

\---

# Category II - Secret Route Endings

These endings require complete commitment.

The player remains consistent through the Thread Masters and the Reclaimer.

The Weaver is defeated.

The path then continues beyond the standard ending.

The Threadborne returns to the Chamber of the First Weave where Thought finally reveals himself.

The Mysterious Follower and Thought are one and the same.

Thought serves as the final secret boss.

Thought intervenes because the player has embraced one of the two extreme answers to the wound.

## White Secret Ending

Requirements:

* Spare all Thread Masters
* Spare the Reclaimer
* Defeat the Weaver
* Defeat Thought

Theme:
Release. Restoration. Letting go.

Thought challenges whether the player truly understands surrendering power rather than merely refusing it.

## Black Secret Ending

Requirements:

* Absorb all Thread Masters
* Absorb the Reclaimer
* Defeat the Weaver
* Defeat Thought

Theme:
Accumulation. Authority. Responsibility.

Thought challenges whether the player has become another Thread Master or something entirely new.

\---

# Category III - Monochromatic Endings

These endings occur when the player absorbs only a single Thread and spares all others.

## Red Ending

The Threadborne becomes the successor to the Monarch.

## Blue Ending

The Threadborne becomes the successor to the Hermit.

## Yellow Ending

The Threadborne becomes the successor to the Sage.

These endings are intentionally bittersweet.
The wound remains.

# Category IV - Hybrid Endings

These endings occur when the player mixes absorb and spare choices without committing to a single philosophy.

No secret route is unlocked.

No succession occurs.

The player defeats the Weaver and the world continues.

The wound remains unresolved.

The future remains uncertain.

Thought does not reveal himself.

He continues to watch.

He continues to wonder.

## Red + Blue

Power and Balance shape the future.

## Red + Yellow

Power and Essence shape the future.

## Blue + Yellow

Balance and Essence shape the future.

\---

# Thought's Role

Thought should not become the new Weaver in any ending.

Thought may guide, challenge, or sacrifice.

He does not seek to rule.

He does not become the world's administrator.
